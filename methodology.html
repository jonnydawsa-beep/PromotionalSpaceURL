Methodology

What has to be true before we call something a deal.

A large price difference is not enough. Retailer feeds can contain bad values, stale stock and different product variants, so publication requires multiple checks.

1

Identify the exact product

We prioritise identifiers such as GTIN/EAN and manufacturer part number. Similar titles alone are not treated as proof that two listings are identical.

2

Use a consistent price basis

If delivery information is reliably available for the compared offers, the comparison can use delivered price. Otherwise it is identified as an item-price comparison.

3

Reject implausible data

Malformed URLs, impossible prices, missing identifiers and other feed-quality problems are not allowed to create public saving claims.

4

Check freshness and stock

An offer must be current enough to publish and should be available to buy when the feed provides stock information. The final retailer page remains authoritative.

5

Describe the evidence honestly

We do not describe an offer as the cheapest on the internet unless the evidence actually supports that claim. A comparison is limited to the retailer data observed by this service.

Price changes: retailer prices and stock can change after our check. Always confirm the final price, delivery and terms on the retailer website before buying.
